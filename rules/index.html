Manual

Rules & How It Works

Uforiya is built around two levels of organisation: projects and tracks. Both have their own collaborators, roles and permissions that work independently of each other. This page explains exactly how everything works so you always know who can do what and why.

01

Uploads & Projects

Every track on Uforiya must belong to a project. When you upload a track you choose to either create a new project or add it to an existing one, including both projects you own and projects you are an editor on.

Every track belongs to a project

You cannot upload a track without assigning it to a project. You can create a new project during upload or add to an existing one you own or edit.

Creating a project

Name, type and visibility are mandatory. Cover image and all other details are optional. Project type must be one of: Single, EP, Album, Mixtape or Playlist.

Uploading a track

Title and visibility are mandatory when uploading a track. Everything else is optional. Supported formats are MP3, WAV, FLAC, AIFF and M4A.

You own every track you upload

Whoever uploads a track is its owner, regardless of their role in the project. Uploading to someone else's project does not give them ownership of your track.

BPM, key, duration and format are detected automatically on upload. You can override BPM and key manually if the detection is incorrect.

02

Collaborators: Two Levels

Uforiya has collaborators at two separate levels: project level and track level. These are managed independently. Adding someone to a project does not automatically add them to any track, and adding someone to a track does not give them project access.

At both levels, a collaborator is either an Editor or a Listener. The permissions for each role differ between project and track level and are explained in the sections below.

Project-level collaborators

Managed from Project Settings. Only the project owner can invite, remove or change the role of a project collaborator. Project collaborators have access to every track in the project, and project editors can do anything a track editor can do on those tracks.

Track-level collaborators

Managed from Track Settings by the track owner or the project owner. Track collaborators are separate from project collaborators and only have access to the track they were invited to.

Who can see the collaborator list

The collaborator list shows who holds which role and who has an invite pending. It is only visible to the owner and to people who were actually invited to the track or project. Someone who arrived through a share link cannot see it.

If you want people to see who worked on a track without showing them the access list, that is what Credits is for. See Section 05.

03

Project Roles

Every person in a project has one of three roles. These roles control what they can do within the project.

RolePermissions
OwnerOwner Full control. Can invite and remove collaborators, delete the project, change project visibility, edit project details, and upload new versions of any track in the project. There is one owner per project.
EditorEditor Can upload tracks to the project and keeps ownership of those tracks. Has the same capabilities as a track editor on every track in the project. Cannot invite or remove collaborators, delete the project, or change project visibility.
ListenerListener Listen access only. Can stream the tracks in the project, current version only. Cannot upload, edit or manage anything.

Project collaborators are invited by username or email. You can invite someone who does not yet have a Uforiya account by email; they will receive an invitation to join.

04

Track Roles

At the track level, collaborators are also either Editors or Listeners. All track editors have the same permissions; there is no permission difference between them.

When you invite an editor you also choose what they did on the track — producer, engineer, songwriter and so on. That choice is their credit, and it appears in the track's Credits. It does not change what they can do.

RolePermissions
OwnerOwner Full control over the track. Can invite and remove track collaborators, mark a version as current, edit track details and credited artists, set track visibility, manage the share link, and decide whether editors can download.
EditorEditor Can upload new versions and stems from Track Settings, and update BPM and key. All editors have the same permissions regardless of their credit.
ListenerListener Listen access only. Can stream the current version of the track and see its credits. Cannot upload, edit, download or manage anything.

Credits are for the record, not for access. A producer and an engineer on the same track have identical editing capabilities. The credit says what they did; the role says what they can do.

05

Credits

Every track has a Credits tab listing everyone who worked on it, grouped by what they did. Credits are the liner notes: anyone who can hear the track can see them, including someone who opened it from a share link with no account.

Credits come from three places and they all appear in the same list.

Credited artists

The artist line under the track title. It starts with the person who uploaded the track, and the track owner can add or remove names. A track must always have at least one credited artist, so you can remove your own name only once someone else is credited.

Collaborators on Uforiya

Every editor you invite is credited by what you invited them as. Change their credit in Collaborators and the Credits list updates with it. Listeners are not credited — they did not work on the track.

People without a Uforiya account

The track owner can add anyone by name from the Credits tab, with the role they played. Use this for people who are not on the platform. If they do have an account, invite them as a collaborator instead so they get access too.

Artist Producer Engineer Songwriter Composer Vocalist Featured Artist Other

On a project page, a track only shows its own artist line when it differs from the project's. If the whole project is by one artist, the header already says so.

06

Versions

Versions are the core of Uforiya. Every time an audio file is uploaded for a track it is saved as a new version. Nothing is overwritten. Your full version history is always there.

Versions are unlimited on every plan

There is no cap on how many versions a track can have, on Free or on Pro. The only ceiling is your storage. See Section 16.

Where versions are uploaded

New versions can only be uploaded from Track Settings. This keeps version history intentional and organised.

Who can upload a new version

The track owner, the project owner, and any project or track editor can upload a new version of a track.

Who marks the current version

Only the track owner can mark a version as current. This ensures nothing is officially changed without the track owner's sign-off, even if editors can upload new files.

Nothing is overwritten

Every version you upload stays on the platform. You can go back to any version at any time. Uploading a new one never removes an old one.

Versions do not count toward your track limit. A track with twenty versions is still one track.

07

Stems

Stems are the individual parts of a mix — drums, bass, vocals, whatever you want to keep separate. On Uforiya stems belong to a version, not to the track as a whole, so you always know which mix a given set of stems came from.

Stems attach to a version

Open a version in Track Settings and add stems to it. Each stem gets a name and a category so the list stays readable.

Carry stems into a new version

When you upload a new version you can bring stems from an earlier one across instead of uploading them again. Nothing is duplicated in storage when you do.

Who can see stems

Only owners and editors. Listeners cannot see or download stems, and neither can anyone who opened the track from a share link.

Size limit

500MB per stem. Stems count toward the storage of whoever uploaded them, same as anything else.

If you upload the same stem file twice to the same track, Uforiya recognises it and links the existing file rather than storing it again.

08

Lyrics

Every track can have lyrics attached to it. Owners and editors can write and edit them at any time.

Who can write them

The track owner, the project owner, and any editor. Lyrics belong to the track, not to a single version, so they stay put when you upload a new mix.

Who can read them

Owners and editors always can. The track owner decides whether listeners see them, with a switch in Access. It is off by default.

Showing lyrics to listeners does not change who can reach the track. It only decides what the people who already have access can read.

09

Visibility

Projects and tracks each have their own visibility, and they are independent of each other. A private project is your workspace; a public track is something you chose to release. You can have both at once.

Private

Only people who were invited can reach it. A private project is not browsable by anyone else and has no share link.

Public

Anyone with the link can listen, with or without an account. A public project also lets anyone with its link browse the public tracks inside it.

A public track in a private project

This works, and it is the normal way to release one song out of a project you are still working on. The track is reachable by its own link. The rest of the project stays private, and the project itself has no link.

Who controls what

The project owner sets the project's visibility. The track owner sets their own track's. Neither one overrides the other.

Making a track private immediately removes everyone who joined it through a public link. People you invited directly keep their access — going private does not undo an invitation.

10

Share Links

Public tracks and public projects each get their own link. Send it to anyone — a manager, a label, a friend — and they can listen straight away. They do not need a Uforiya account.

What someone with the link can do

Listen to the current version, and see the title, artwork and credits. That is all. They cannot download, see other versions, see stems, read hidden lyrics, or see anything else in the project.

Adding it to your library

Someone with a Uforiya account who opens your link can save it to their own library to keep it and get updates. They are still a listener — saving something does not give them any extra access.

Turning a link off and on

Making a track or project private kills its link immediately. Making it public again brings the same link back, so anything you already shared starts working again.

Generating a new link

If a link ends up somewhere you did not intend, generate a new one. This cannot be undone. Every copy of the old link stops working the moment you do it, and the old one can never be recovered.

Links never contain anything guessable about your account or your other music. Making something private and generating a new link are two different tools: one is a pause, the other is permanent.

11

Downloads

Downloading gives someone the actual audio file, so it is held tighter than listening.

Owners can always download

The track owner and the project owner can download any version of a track at any time.

Editors only when it is switched on

The track owner decides whether editors can download. It is off by default — turn it on in Track Settings when you want your collaborators to have the files.

Listeners never can

Listeners and anyone using a share link can only stream. There is no setting that changes this.

Stems follow the same rule as versions. Only people who can see stems at all — owners and editors — can download them.

12

Track Ownership & Standalone Tracks

Track ownership always stays with whoever uploaded the track. There are several situations where a track can be separated from a project and become a standalone single.

Project is deleted

If a project owner deletes a project, tracks uploaded by editors within that project are not deleted. They become standalone projects with the type Single, still owned by the original track owner.

Project owner removes a track

The project owner can remove a track uploaded by an editor from their project. The removed track becomes a standalone Single owned by the original track owner.

Track owner removes their own track

A track owner can remove their own track from a project at any time. This applies only when the project is not owned by the track owner. The track becomes a standalone Single under their ownership.

Track ownership never transfers automatically. Transferring ownership to someone else is not available in the current version of Uforiya but is planned for a future release.

13

Deleting Things

Deleting is deliberate on Uforiya — you will always be asked to confirm. But mistakes happen, and we would rather get your music back than be strict about it.

Who can delete what

Only the person who uploaded a track can delete it. A project owner who did not upload it can remove it from their project instead, which turns it into a standalone single rather than destroying it.

Deleted something by accident?

Email support@uforiya.com with your username and the name of what you lost. We cannot promise to get everything back, but in most cases we can recover it within 30 days of deletion. After that, assume it is gone.

Deleting a project deletes the tracks you uploaded to it. Tracks other people uploaded survive as standalone singles — see Section 12.

14

Free Audio Tools

Uforiya has seven audio tools you can use on any file, whether or not it is on the platform: Trim, Fade, Convert, Compress, Pitch, Tempo and Normalize.

No account needed for four of them

Trim, Fade, Convert and Compress work with no sign-up at all. Drop a file in, get it back.

A free account adds the rest

Signing up unlocks Pitch, Tempo and Normalize, raises your daily limit, and lets you send a result straight into one of your projects.

Daily limits

There is a daily cap on how many files you can process. It is higher with an account and higher again on Pro.

Files you put through the tools are not added to your library and do not count toward your storage unless you choose to send the result into a project.

15

Accounts

When you need an account

You need one to upload, collaborate, or keep anything in a library. You do not need one to open a share link someone sent you, or to use the four tools listed in Section 14.

Free to start

Creating an account is free. The Free plan gives you 25 tracks, unlimited versions, 10GB of storage and unlimited collaborators. No credit card required.

Uforiya is in beta

It is open to everyone and free to start. Things will change as we go, and the limits below can move.

16

Plans & Storage

There are two plans. Collaboration is on both of them — unlimited collaborators, all seven audio tools, and the full version history, whether you pay or not. What you pay for is room.

PlanTracksVersionsStorageCollaborators
Free25Unlimited10GBUnlimited
ProUnlimitedUnlimited1TBUnlimited

Storage follows whoever uploaded the file

Everything you upload counts toward your own storage — the original track, every version, every stem — no matter whose project or whose track it went to. If you upload a version to a collaborator's track, that file is on your storage, not theirs.

Why it works that way

The person who picks the file is the person who pays for it. It also means nobody can fill up your storage by uploading large files to your project.

The track limit works the same way

Your track limit counts every track you upload, whichever project it ends up in. If you upload to someone else's project it counts for you; if someone uploads to yours, it counts for them.

Versions never count as tracks

A track with fifty versions is one track. Only the storage adds up.

If you run out of storage you cannot upload anything new — including to someone else's track — until you free some space or upgrade. Everything already uploaded stays exactly where it is.

Pro is $12.99/month or $99.99/year, in Canadian dollars. The yearly price works out around 36% cheaper. Pro also adds daily, weekly and monthly analytics and finer control over downloads.
